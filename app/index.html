A shrinking population

Japan's population peaked at 128.1 million in 2010 and has fallen every year since. In 2024 it stood at 124.0 million, down 0.4% on the year. Births fell to 727,277 in 2023 while deaths reached 1,590,503 — a natural decrease of more than 860,000 people in a single year.

Total population, millions. Source: World Bank, World Development Indicators.

The economy

Nominal GDP was $4.19 trillion in 2024, the world's fourth largest. In yen terms the economy kept growing; in dollars it shrank, because the yen weakened.

Nominal GDP, billion US dollars. Source: World Bank WDI.

An ageing country

Almost three in ten people in Japan are aged 65 or older — the highest share of any large country. Children under 15 are 11.4% of the population.

Where people live

Tokyo holds 14.18 million people — 11.5% of the country. Together with Kanagawa, Saitama and Chiba, the Greater Tokyo area holds 29.9% of Japan.

Population, millions, 1 October 2024. Source: Statistics Bureau of Japan, Population Estimates.

Japan in the world

Japan is the world's 4th-largest economy by nominal GDP and its 12th most populous country. Its life expectancy of 84.0 years ranks 10th in the world, and 29.8% of its people are aged 65 or older — the highest share of any large country. The pages of this atlas put those headlines in context: the Population page follows the shrinking, ageing population; the Economy page follows output, trade and work; and the Regions page ranks all 47 prefectures.
